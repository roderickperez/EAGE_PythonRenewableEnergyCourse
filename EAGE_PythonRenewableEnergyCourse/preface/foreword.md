# Prologue

As of March 2026, the world is living through two major transformations at the same time: the acceleration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and the restructuring of the global energy system. Both are deeply connected. AI is increasing our ability to model, predict, optimize, and automate; energy systems are being pushed to become cleaner, more resilient, and more efficient.

## The Current State of AI (March 2026)

AI has moved from experimental tools to operational infrastructure in many industries. Large language models and multimodal systems are now used for software development, technical documentation, customer support, scientific workflows, and decision support. In engineering and geoscience contexts, AI is increasingly applied to data quality control, forecasting, classification, anomaly detection, and workflow automation.

At the same time, three realities define the current AI moment:

1. **Productivity gains are real, but uneven.** Organizations with strong data practices and clear problem definitions benefit the most.
2. **Model quality is improving, but reliability is still a challenge.** Hallucinations, bias, and explainability remain key limitations in high-stakes workflows.
3. **Governance is now essential.** Topics such as data privacy, model transparency, intellectual property, security, and energy use of AI systems are central to responsible deployment.

In short, AI in 2026 is powerful and practical, but it still requires human expertise, domain knowledge, and careful validation.

## The Current Global Energy Situation

The global energy system remains dominated by fossil fuels, but the transition toward low-carbon energy continues to accelerate.

### Main Energy Sources

The current global mix is still led by:

- Oil
- Coal
- Natural gas

At the same time, low-carbon sources continue expanding:

- Hydropower
- Wind
- Solar
- Nuclear
- Bioenergy and other renewables

Electricity systems in many countries are seeing fast growth in variable renewables (especially solar and wind), while thermal generation remains important for reliability and industrial demand.

### Main Producing Regions and Countries

Energy production remains geographically concentrated:

- **Oil and gas:** major production from the United States, Middle East producers (including Saudi Arabia and the UAE), Russia, and other key exporting countries.
- **Coal:** significant production and consumption in Asia, especially China and India.
- **Hydropower:** large contributions from countries with abundant water resources (for example China, Brazil, Canada, and parts of Europe).
- **Wind and solar:** strongest growth in China, the United States, the European Union, and India, with additional momentum in Latin America, Africa, and Southeast Asia.

### Structural Trends Defining 2026

1. **Electrification is expanding** in transport, buildings, and parts of industry.
2. **Grid modernization is now critical,** including storage, transmission expansion, and digital monitoring.
3. **Energy security and affordability remain strategic priorities,** especially after recent geopolitical and market disruptions.
4. **Decarbonization commitments are driving policy and investment,** but progress differs by country and sector.
