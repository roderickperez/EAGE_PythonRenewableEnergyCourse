# Another source: U.S. EIA electricity data

The [U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) electricity data](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/data.php) provides public U.S. electricity statistics, including generation. It complements the [Eurostat monthly exercise](download-to-database.md) and [worldwide annual dataset](world-energy-download.md); it is a data provider, not a PostgreSQL or SQLite server. Downloaded observations can be stored in your own database.

## A practical workflow

1. Open EIA's [Electricity Data Browser](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/data/browser/) and choose a generation series, geography, fuel and period. Inspect its units and metadata, then download a CSV.
2. Read the downloaded file with `pd.read_csv`, inspect its actual column names and missing values, and retain the source URL, series name, unit, geography, retrieval date and any revision notes. EIA formats may change; do not assume a CSV schema without inspecting it.
3. Normalize the selected columns, check that the intended observation key is unique, and store the result with `df.to_sql`. Query it with the methods in the [Python and SQLite notebook](sql-python.ipynb). Make a new database or table for the exercise rather than replacing a valuable existing one.
4. Compare like with like: the EIA U.S. series, Eurostat countries and OWID annual aggregates may have different geographic boundaries, reporting periods, units, categories and definitions. Convert units explicitly and never add country records to an aggregate containing them.

EIA's [net generation](https://www.eia.gov/tools/glossary/index.php?id=net+generation) subtracts station use from gross generation, including electricity used for pumped storage. It is not the same as [net electricity consumption](https://www.eia.gov/tools/glossary/index.php?id=Net+electricity+consumption), whose accounting also includes imports, exports and transmission and distribution losses. A pumped-storage net-generation value can be negative; decide validation rules from the selected measure rather than assuming all generation series are nonnegative. Dataset-specific reporting and imputation rules must also be checked before comparing series.

If the Data Browser export is unavailable, the [Monthly Energy Review table 7.2a CSV](https://www.eia.gov/totalenergy/data/browser/csv.php?tbl=T07.02A), linked from the [EIA table catalogue](https://www.eia.gov/totalenergy/data/monthly/), is an alternative for this exercise. Inspect its series descriptions, units and table notes: the solar series covers utility-scale generation, including solar thermal and PV, but excludes small-scale PV. In the checked CSV, `YYYY13` denotes an annual record and `YYYY01`–`YYYY12` monthly records; select one time basis and do not sum an annual record together with its months. Do not add a source to a total that already includes it. Record the retrieval date because EIA revises its data.

For automated retrieval, EIA's [API documentation](https://www.eia.gov/opendata/documentation.php) describes the v2 routes, filters and pagination. Its API requires a free registered key in the request URL. Read the key from an environment variable or other secret store; do not commit it to a notebook or share the complete request URL. Before saving or sharing logs, provenance, notebook output or raw responses, inspect and redact credentials: API responses can include request parameters as well as data. Record the route, filters, retrieval date and a clearly identified checksum of the appropriately protected or redacted artifact without recording the key. Browse the route metadata and inspect units before writing an automated query. If the request fails or access is unavailable, use the documented manual download; do not substitute invented observations.
